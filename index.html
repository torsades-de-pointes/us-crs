How the score is calculated

US-CRS 2.0 is a weighted pooled logistic (discrete-time) model of death or delisting for deterioration within 6 weeks, fitted as a marginal structural model so that it estimates risk in a world without transplant. The linear predictor is the log-odds of the weekly hazard.

LP = 7.45 − 0.837 × albumin (g/dL) + 0.911 × ln(bilirubin + 1) − 0.00862 × eGFR + 0.178 × ln(NP + 1) − 1.449 × NT + 0.139 × ln(NP + 1) × NT − 0.07954 × sodium − 0.43 × durable LVAD + 0.757 × IABP + 0.75 × microaxial flow pump + 1.676 × advanced MCS NP = BNP or NT-proBNP value; NT = 1 if NT-proBNP, 0 if BNP.

From linear predictor to the 50-point score

The paper maps the linear predictor to integers 1–50 by equal-width bins (eTable 16): a score of 50 is LP above −3.5457, a score of 1 is LP at or below −9.4417, and each point in between spans about 0.1228 log-odds. The 6-Status US-CRS tiers used in the paper's match-run analysis are: tier 1 = 50, tier 2 = 45–49, tier 3 = 40–44, tier 4 = 30–39, tier 5 = 20–29, tier 6 = 1–19.

Estimated 6-week mortality

The calculator converts the weekly hazard h = 1 / (1 + e−LP) to 6-week risk as 1 − (1 − h)6. This is an approximation of the model's prediction. The authors report that the model over-estimates risk in the highest-risk candidates, so treat values above about 20% as relative, not absolute.

Trimming and missing values

As in the paper, values are trimmed to the training data's 1st and 99th percentiles (eTable 3), and missing labs are replaced with the national sample median (eTable 2). The calculator flags any value that was trimmed or imputed.

Performance in the paper

In held-out test data (September 2024 to August 2025), mean weighted time-dependent AUC was 0.807 for US-CRS 2.0 vs 0.693 for the current 6-status system. The score was well calibrated for candidates listed by exception.

Known limitations

  • No hemodynamics, diagnosis, age or sex in the score; restrictive, infiltrative and congenital disease are scored on the same labs.
  • Lab caps flatten the top of the scale (for example, bilirubin above 2.8 mg/dL adds nothing more).
  • NT-proBNP runs lower with obesity and in Black adults, which may lower the score for some patients.
  • Device points depend on access to devices; the score has not been validated across social-risk groups.
  • The score predicts urgency only; it says nothing about expected benefit after transplant.

About

A free, open, client-side calculator for the US Candidate Risk Score 2.0, built for the Advanced Heart Failure and Transplant Cardiology program at UAB as a teaching and research aid for transplant coordinators, attendings and fellows.

Source

Lyden GR, White M, Ahn DJ, Menteer J, Daly RC, Narang N, Khush KK, Jasseron C, Dorent R, Masotti M, Wood NL, Colvin MM, Gentry SE, Parker WF. Development and validation of an adult medical urgency score for US donor heart allocation. JAMA. 2026;336(18):1621–1629. doi:10.1001/jama.2026.17403

Install on your phone

  • iPhone (Safari): tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android (Chrome): tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.

Once installed, the calculator works offline. Phones open the mobile version automatically; the desktop version is one tap away in its Info tab.

Privacy

Everything runs in your browser. No values are saved, uploaded or logged. Do not enter patient names, MRNs or dates of birth.

Roadmap

  • v2: area-level social context from ZIP code (Area Deprivation Index, Social Vulnerability Index, rurality) shown alongside the score.
  • v2: count of adverse social determinants, for research on whether social risk modifies score performance.
  • v3: serial scores over time (trajectory), and a lab-timing view aligned to status forms.
